# About Michael Garcia

I’m a robotics engineer and product development lead. I design electromechanical systems and the software that operates them, and I help engineering teams move prototypes into manufacturing. At M&E Design, I lead research and development for projects that span embedded devices, autonomous systems, and training products. My work often starts with a physical task and two questions. What should the system do, and how will we know it can do it reliably?

## Robotics and embedded systems

I work across sensors, controls, embedded software, and mechanical assemblies. For the Smart Locket, I prototyped a wearable that lets approved contributors send photos through a mobile app. I’ve also built a modular plant-watering system that measures soil moisture at each plant, requests watering as needed, and records watering history on a local web page. Both projects put a user need in charge of the electronics and software choices.

[See the plant-watering system on GitHub](https://github.com/MDGarcia1985/HousePlantWateringSystem)

## Software and engineering workflows

At Viking Group, I led changes to how engineering requests, prototypes, and revisions moved through SOLIDWORKS PDM. I worked with Engineering, Manufacturing, Quality, Supply Chain, and Operations to clarify who owned each decision and what had to be reviewed before release. The work helped cut the engineering change request backlog by more than half in 18 months.

I bring the same interest in traceability to Captain’s Log, an engineering and personal journaling app I’m developing to keep observations, photos, and related notes easy to find as a project evolves.

[Explore Captain’s Log on GitHub](https://github.com/MDGarcia1985/Captains_Log)

## Product design and fabrication

At Encoris, I designed surgical trainers that had to feel realistic in use and be practical to manufacture. For a spinal curvature correction trainer, I developed a foam formulation that approximated how bone responds during drilling and screw placement. I also redesigned the parts used up during practice as low-cost consumables so the rest of the trainer could be reused.

I use prototypes to answer specific questions early. That might mean checking how a material behaves under a tool, whether a sensor provides useful feedback, or whether an assembly can be built and serviced as designed. I document those decisions so the next person can build on the work.
